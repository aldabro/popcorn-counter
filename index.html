    Tips & how it works

    Getting a good count

    • Put the phone 20–50 cm from the pot, or beside the microwave. Not on the stove or on top of the microwave, because it gets hot.
    • Start listening before the first pop. It learns the background noise within a second.
    • Steady noise (extractor fan, microwave hum) is filtered out, and so is most talking. Short, sharp sounds still count as pops: cutlery, dishes, chopping, running water, sizzling in another pan. Keep those away from the phone while it's popping.
    • Watch the microphone meter: pops should jump past the orange tick, background noise should stay below it. Adjust the sensitivity if they don't.
    • Keep this page open and in front. It keeps the screen awake where the browser allows it.

    How it counts

    A pop is a very short, sharp burst of sound, only a few milliseconds long. The app filters out low-frequency rumble, keeps track of the background noise level, and counts a pop whenever the sound jumps far above that level within a few milliseconds.

    Two pops less than about 10 ms apart count as one. In the busiest seconds of a big batch the count can run a bit low. That's why it doesn't only rely on the percentage.

    Talking is tricky: every vowel is a stream of tiny clicks from the vocal cords, 100 to 250 per second. The app recognises voices by their pitch. While someone talks, it only counts sounds that look like pops rather than voice clicks: bright and standing out from the voice. Some pops are missed while people talk, and a few voice sounds still get through. Without this filter, someone talking next to the phone can add dozens to hundreds of false pops per minute.

    Other short, sharp sounds, like cutlery, chopping or water splashing, are physically very similar to a pop. Often they are even sharper. The app can't reliably tell them apart by sound alone.

    When it tells you to stop

    The alarm goes off when the first of these happens:

    • Kernel share: the set percentage of your kernels has popped.
    • Slow-down: after the peak, a gap between pops reaches your set length (2.5 s by default). Small batches never pop very fast, so they need a somewhat longer gap.
    • Popping curve: popping follows an S-shaped curve over time. Once the peak has passed, the app fits that curve to your pops and estimates how far through the batch you are, without needing the exact kernel count.

    Where the numbers come from

    • Supplier spec sheets list butterfly popcorn at about 50–74 kernels per 10 g (for example 63–70, 68–74), and mushroom popcorn at about 42–62 (for example 42–58). The app uses 6.5 and 5.2 kernels per gram. For the best accuracy, count your own.
    • Microwave bags: the weight on the label includes oil or butter and salt. Labels show roughly 15–35% fat, so the app assumes about 70% of the weight is kernels.
    • Not every kernel pops. A Purdue study of 14 brands found 4% to 47% of kernels left unpopped, so 100% is never reached.
